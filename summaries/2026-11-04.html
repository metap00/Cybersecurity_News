💡 Today I Learned: Cryptographic Agility ▶
Cryptographic agility is the design principle of building systems so they can swap out encryption algorithms — like AES, RSA, or SHA-256 — without having to rewrite the entire application. This matters enormously right now because quantum computers are expected to break widely used public-key algorithms, and standards bodies like NIST are finalising post-quantum replacements (ML-KEM, ML-DSA). Systems that hardcoded a single algorithm will face expensive rewrites, while crypto-agile systems can migrate by updating a configuration value.
📖 Glossary: SIEM · MFA · PKI ▶
SIEM — Security Information and Event Management — a platform that aggregates and correlates log data from across an environment to surface suspicious patterns and alert defenders in real time.
MFA — Multi-Factor Authentication — a security control that requires two or more independent proofs of identity (something you know, something you have, or something you are) before granting access.
PKI — Public Key Infrastructure — the framework of certificate authorities, digital certificates, and policies that underpins encrypted web traffic (HTTPS), code signing, and email encryption.
