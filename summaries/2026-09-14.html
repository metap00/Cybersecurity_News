💡 Today I Learned: Passkey ▶
A passkey is a phishing-resistant credential that replaces passwords using public-key cryptography: your device holds a private key that never leaves it, and the website only stores the matching public key. When you log in, your device proves possession of the private key by signing a challenge — no shared secret is ever transmitted over the network, so there's nothing for an attacker to steal via phishing or server breach. Passkeys are built on the FIDO2/WebAuthn standard and are already supported by Apple, Google, and Microsoft, making them the most practical path toward a passwordless future for everyday users.
📖 Glossary: Passkey · DMARC · MFA · OPSEC · PKI ▶
Passkey — A phishing-resistant login credential using public-key cryptography, replacing passwords with a device-bound key pair standardized under FIDO2/WebAuthn.
DMARC — Domain-based Message Authentication, Reporting & Conformance; an email policy protocol that tells receiving mail servers how to handle messages that fail SPF or DKIM checks, helping block spoofed "from" addresses.
MFA — Multi-Factor Authentication; the practice of requiring two or more verification factors (something you know, have, or are) before granting access, significantly reducing account-takeover risk.
OPSEC — Operational Security; the process of identifying and protecting sensitive information that, if collected by adversaries, could reveal plans, capabilities, or intentions.
PKI — Public Key Infrastructure; the system of certificates, certificate authorities, and policies that enables parties to verify each other's identities using asymmetric cryptography.
