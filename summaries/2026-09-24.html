💡 Today I Learned: Network Segmentation ▶
Network segmentation is the practice of dividing a computer network into smaller, isolated sub-networks called segments or zones, so that a breach in one area cannot automatically spread to the rest of the organization. For example, a hospital might place its medical devices on a separate segment from its administrative systems, connected only through tightly controlled gateways, so that ransomware hitting the billing department cannot reach life-critical monitors. Common tools for segmentation include VLANs (Virtual Local Area Networks) and physical firewalls, with the most sensitive assets often placed inside a DMZ or even fully air-gapped from internet-facing systems.
📖 Glossary: VLAN · DMZ ▶
VLAN — Virtual Local Area Network; a logical grouping of network devices that behave as if they share a physical switch even though they may be spread across different hardware, used to isolate traffic between departments or system types.
DMZ — Demilitarized Zone; a network segment that sits between the public internet and an organization's internal trusted network, hosting internet-facing services like web servers while keeping them separated from sensitive internal systems.
