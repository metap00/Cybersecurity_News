💡 Today I Learned: Canary Tokens ▶
A canary token is a unique, trackable resource — a URL, file, credential, or code snippet — deliberately planted in a system, document, or repository so that any unauthorized access triggers an automatic alert to the defender. When an attacker opens the decoy file, visits the URL, or attempts to use the credential, the token "fires" and reports back details such as the attacker's IP address and the time of access, giving defenders early warning deep inside a compromise. Because canary tokens cost almost nothing to deploy yet provide high-fidelity signals, they are one of the most effective and underused tools in a blue team's arsenal for detecting intrusions that have bypassed perimeter defenses.
📖 Glossary: Canary Token · Honeypot · OSINT · TTP ▶
Canary Token — A trackable decoy resource (URL, file, credential) planted in systems to silently alert defenders when an attacker accesses it.
Honeypot — A deliberately vulnerable or attractive decoy system designed to lure attackers and observe their methods without risk to real assets.
OSINT — Open-Source Intelligence; the collection and analysis of information gathered from publicly available sources such as social media, domain records, and leaked databases.
TTP — Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures; the specific behaviors and methods used by a threat actor, mapped in frameworks like MITRE ATT&CK to characterize how an attack unfolds.
