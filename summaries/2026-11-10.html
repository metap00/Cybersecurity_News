💡 Today I Learned: Kerberoasting ▶
Kerberoasting is an Active Directory attack where an adversary requests Kerberos service tickets for accounts with Service Principal Names (SPNs), then extracts those tickets and cracks them offline to recover the service account's plaintext password. Because any authenticated domain user can request a service ticket without special privileges, this technique is stealthy and hard to detect at request time. Organizations defend against it by ensuring service account passwords are long and random (over 25 characters), using Group Managed Service Accounts (gMSA), and monitoring for unusual Kerberos TGS-REQ traffic patterns in a SIEM.
📖 Glossary: SPN · TGT · gMSA ▶
SPN — Service Principal Name; a unique identifier for a service instance in Active Directory, used by Kerberos authentication to associate a service with an account.
TGT — Ticket Granting Ticket; a Kerberos credential issued by the Key Distribution Center (KDC) after initial login, used to request service tickets without re-entering credentials.
gMSA — Group Managed Service Account; a Windows account type that automatically rotates its own complex password, making it highly resistant to Kerberoasting attacks.
