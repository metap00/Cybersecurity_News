💡 Today I Learned: DNS Sinkholes ▶
A DNS sinkhole is a defensive technique where malicious domain names used by malware are intentionally redirected to a controlled server operated by security researchers, ISPs, or law enforcement instead of the attacker's real infrastructure. When infected machines try to reach their command-and-control server using the malicious domain name, they connect to the sinkhole instead, effectively cutting off communication between the malware and its operators. Sinkholes are widely used to neutralize botnets at scale — by seizing or spoofing key domains, researchers can simultaneously disrupt thousands or millions of infected devices and gather intelligence on infection rates without touching the devices themselves.
📖 Glossary: C2 · IOC · DNS Sinkhole ▶
C2 (Command and Control) — The infrastructure — servers, domains, and channels — that attackers use to remotely direct and receive data from compromised systems.
IOC (Indicator of Compromise) — A forensic artifact such as a malicious IP address, domain, file hash, or registry key that indicates a system has been compromised.
DNS Sinkhole — A controlled server that intercepts and absorbs DNS queries destined for attacker-controlled domains, cutting off malware from its operators.
