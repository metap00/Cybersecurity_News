💡 Today I Learned: Certificate Transparency ▶
Certificate Transparency (CT) is a system that requires every TLS certificate to be publicly recorded in append-only, cryptographically verifiable logs called CT Logs before browsers will trust it. Each log is structured as a Merkle tree — a data structure that lets anyone efficiently prove whether a specific certificate is in the log and that the log has never been altered. This means if a certificate authority is tricked or compromised into issuing a fake certificate for your domain, you (and automated monitors) can detect it within hours rather than waiting months or years for the fraud to surface on its own.
📖 Glossary: CT Log · Merkle Tree ▶
CT Log — a publicly auditable, append-only ledger that records every TLS/SSL certificate ever submitted to it, enabling detection of misissued or fraudulent certificates.
Merkle Tree — a cryptographic data structure where every leaf node contains a data hash and every parent node contains the combined hash of its children, allowing tamper-evident verification of large datasets with minimal data transfer.
