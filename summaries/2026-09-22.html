💡 Today I Learned: Memory-Safe Languages ▶
Memory-safe languages like Rust, Go, and Swift are designed so the compiler or runtime prevents entire classes of vulnerabilities — such as buffer overflows, use-after-free errors, and null-pointer dereferences — by enforcing strict rules about how memory is accessed and when it is freed. These bugs account for roughly 70% of critical vulnerabilities found in software written in C and C++, languages that manage memory manually and rely entirely on the programmer to avoid mistakes. In 2023, CISA and the NSA jointly urged software manufacturers to transition to memory-safe languages, recognizing that security tooling alone cannot eliminate risks that are baked into the language's fundamental design.
📖 Glossary: Supply Chain Attack · IR · MitM ▶
Supply Chain Attack — An attack that compromises software, hardware, or services upstream of the intended target (for example, poisoning a widely-used open-source library) so that every downstream consumer becomes a victim without being directly targeted.
IR (Incident Response) — The structured process an organization follows to detect, contain, eradicate, and recover from a security incident, ending with a post-mortem to improve defenses for the future.
MitM (Man-in-the-Middle) — An attack where an adversary secretly intercepts and can read or alter the communications between two parties who each believe they are talking directly and privately to each other.
