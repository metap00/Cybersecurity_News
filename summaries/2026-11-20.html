💡 Today I Learned: Lateral Movement ▶
Lateral movement is how an attacker, once inside a network, navigates from one system to another to expand their foothold, escalate privileges, or reach high-value targets — rather than attacking from outside, they "blend in" by exploiting legitimate credentials, remote management tools, and trust relationships between internal systems. This makes detection harder because the traffic looks like normal administrative activity, which is why security teams monitor east-west (internal) traffic just as carefully as north-south (inbound/outbound) traffic. Defenders counter lateral movement with network segmentation, the principle of least privilege, and behavioral analytics that flag unusual patterns such as a workstation suddenly connecting to a database server it has never touched before.
📖 Glossary: TTPs · IOC · EDR ▶
TTPs — Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures; the behavioral profile of a threat actor describing how they operate, from high-level goals (tactics) to specific methods (techniques) to detailed step-by-step implementations (procedures).
IOC — Indicator of Compromise; observable evidence on a network or host — such as a suspicious IP address, file hash, or unexpected registry key — that suggests a system may have been breached or is under active attack.
EDR — Endpoint Detection and Response; a security solution that continuously monitors endpoints like laptops and servers, records activity, and uses behavioral analytics to detect, investigate, and respond to advanced threats in near real-time.
