💡 Today I Learned: Supply Chain Attack ▶
A supply chain attack targets software or hardware before it reaches the end user — instead of attacking a well-defended company directly, adversaries compromise a trusted vendor, build tool, or update mechanism that the target relies on. The most famous example is the 2020 SolarWinds incident, where attackers slipped malicious code into a software update that was then distributed to thousands of organizations worldwide, including US government agencies. Defending against supply chain attacks requires verifying the integrity of third-party components (using checksums and code signing), monitoring for unexpected network behavior from trusted software, and applying the principle of least privilege so that even a compromised vendor cannot access everything.
📖 Glossary: IoC · OSINT · EDR · C2 · TTP ▶
IoC (Indicator of Compromise) — A piece of forensic evidence — such as a suspicious IP address, file hash, or domain — that suggests a system may have been breached or is communicating with a threat actor.
OSINT (Open-Source Intelligence) — Intelligence gathered from publicly available sources — websites, social media, public records, and leaked databases — used by both defenders and attackers to research targets.
EDR (Endpoint Detection and Response) — Security software installed on individual devices (endpoints) that continuously monitors for suspicious activity and can automatically isolate or remediate threats.
C2 (Command and Control) — The infrastructure — servers, domains, or communication channels — used by an attacker to remotely send instructions to malware already installed on a victim's system.
TTP (Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures) — The characteristic behaviors and methods used by a threat actor, documented in frameworks like MITRE ATT&CK to help defenders recognize and counter specific adversaries.
