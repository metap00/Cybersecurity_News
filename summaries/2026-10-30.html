💡 Today I Learned: SBOM ▶
An SBOM — Software Bill of Materials — is a complete, machine-readable inventory of every open-source library, third-party component, and dependency bundled inside a piece of software, much like a food label lists every ingredient in a package. When a new vulnerability is discovered in a popular library (say, Log4j), an SBOM lets defenders immediately query their entire portfolio to find every product that contains that library — without waiting for vendors to publish advisories. The US government now requires SBOMs for software sold to federal agencies, and the EU's Cyber Resilience Act is pushing similar mandates globally, making them a growing compliance and supply-chain security cornerstone.
📖 Glossary: SBOM · CRA · Supply-Chain Security ▶
SBOM — Software Bill of Materials; a structured list of all components, libraries, and dependencies that make up a software product.
CRA — Cyber Resilience Act; EU regulation requiring manufacturers to ensure cybersecurity throughout a product's lifecycle, including mandatory SBOMs for connected devices.
Supply-Chain Security — Practices aimed at protecting software or hardware from being tampered with or compromised during development, distribution, or updates before it reaches end users.
