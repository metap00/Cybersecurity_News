💡 Today I Learned: Threat Modeling ▶
Threat modeling is a structured process where developers and security teams map out an application's attack surface — every entry point, data flow, and trust boundary — then systematically ask "what could go wrong?" The goal is to find weaknesses before attackers do, ranking risks by likelihood and impact so the team knows where to focus defenses. Frameworks like STRIDE (Spoofing, Tampering, Repudiation, Information Disclosure, Denial of Service, Elevation of Privilege) give teams a checklist of threat categories to work through systematically at design time, long before any code is shipped.
📖 Glossary: MITRE ATT&CK · CVE · CVSS · Kill Chain ▶
MITRE ATT&CK — A publicly available knowledge base of adversary tactics and techniques based on real-world observations, widely used to classify, describe, and communicate attacker behavior across the industry.
CVE — Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures; a standardized identifier (e.g., CVE-2026-12345) assigned to each publicly disclosed software security flaw so vendors, researchers, and defenders share a common reference.
CVSS — Common Vulnerability Scoring System; a 0–10 numerical scale rating a vulnerability's severity based on factors like exploitability, attack complexity, required privileges, and potential impact.
Kill Chain — A military-derived model adapted by Lockheed Martin for cyber threats; it describes the seven stages an attacker typically moves through: Reconnaissance, Weaponization, Delivery, Exploitation, Installation, Command & Control, and Actions on Objectives.
