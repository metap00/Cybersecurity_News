💡 Today I Learned: Side-Channel Attack ▶
A side-channel attack extracts secret information not by breaking a cryptographic algorithm directly, but by analyzing indirect physical signals the hardware produces while computing — such as timing differences, power consumption fluctuations, electromagnetic emissions, or even acoustic noise. For example, by precisely measuring how long a decryption operation takes, an attacker can deduce a secret key bit-by-bit because different key values cause the CPU to follow subtly different execution paths that take measurably different amounts of time. Defending against side-channel attacks requires constant-time code, power-balancing hardware design, and physical shielding — making them a favorite research topic at the fascinating intersection of physics, hardware engineering, and cryptography.
📖 Glossary: TTP · OSINT · SSRF ▶
TTP — Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures; the documented behavioral patterns and methods an attacker uses, as catalogued in frameworks like MITRE ATT&CK.
OSINT — Open Source Intelligence; information gathered exclusively from publicly available sources (social media, WHOIS records, job postings, news) to profile targets or investigate threats without any intrusion.
SSRF — Server-Side Request Forgery; a vulnerability where an attacker tricks a server into making HTTP requests to internal network resources or cloud metadata endpoints on the attacker's behalf.
