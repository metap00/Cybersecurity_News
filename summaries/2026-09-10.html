💡 Today I Learned: Typosquatting ▶
Typosquatting is the practice of registering domain names that are deliberate misspellings or close visual variations of legitimate websites — for example, "arnazon.com" instead of "amazon.com" — banking on the fact that users occasionally hit the wrong key. Attackers use these lookalike domains to host phishing pages, distribute malware, or silently collect credentials from visitors who notice nothing unusual about a page that looks identical to the real one. Organizations defend against typosquatting by proactively registering common misspellings of their own domains, monitoring new domain registrations for brand look-alikes through services like DNSDB or DomainTools, and configuring browsers and email gateways to warn users about newly registered or suspicious domains.
📖 Glossary: DGA · IOC · WAF · APT ▶
DGA — Domain Generation Algorithm; a technique built into malware that automatically generates hundreds of pseudo-random domain names each day so that even if defenders block some command-and-control addresses, the malware can find new ones to phone home to.
IOC — Indicator of Compromise; a forensic artifact — such as a malicious IP address, file hash, domain name, or registry key — whose presence in a system or network logs suggests that an intrusion has occurred or is underway.
WAF — Web Application Firewall; a security appliance or cloud service that sits in front of a web application and inspects incoming HTTP/HTTPS requests, blocking common attack patterns like SQL injection, cross-site scripting, and path traversal before they reach the app.
APT — Advanced Persistent Threat; a sophisticated, long-duration cyberattack campaign — typically attributed to nation-state actors or well-resourced criminal groups — designed to quietly infiltrate a target network and maintain access for months or years while exfiltrating data or pre-positioning for sabotage.
