💡 Today I Learned: Canary Token ▶
A canary token is a digital tripwire — a unique, trackable link, file, or credential embedded in a system or document that silently alerts defenders the moment someone accesses it. When an attacker opens the canary token (a booby-trapped PDF, a fake AWS key in a config file, or a URL hidden in a document), it phones home and tells you exactly who opened it and from where. Security teams plant canary tokens across their environments to catch intruders who have already breached the perimeter and are quietly exploring — the token fires before any real damage is done.
📖 Glossary: Canary Token · Honeypot · TTPs · OSINT ▶
Canary Token — A hidden, trackable digital tripwire (a URL, file, or credential) that alerts defenders the moment an attacker touches it.
Honeypot — A decoy system or resource designed to attract attackers and observe their techniques without exposing real assets.
TTPs — Tactics, Techniques, and Procedures; the behaviors and methods that characterize how a specific threat actor operates.
OSINT — Open Source Intelligence; information gathered from publicly available sources such as social media, public records, and news to support security investigations.
